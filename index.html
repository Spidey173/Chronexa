📊 Architecture & Implementation Decisions

Technical analysis comparing Structured Lexical & Hybrid RRF with generic dense vector search on workplace communication data.

🚨 Why Pure Dense Retrieval Fails on Workplace Data (and why Chronexa fuses Lexical + Dense)

  • Version & Date Blindness: Dense vector models yield high cosine similarity (~0.95 measured with BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5) between "Route Planner v1" and "Route Planner v2", failing to distinguish historical version updates or exact dates.
  • Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) Confusion: Corporate identifiers (e.g. MTG-0916-GONOGO#0135, U03SARAHK, ticket numbers, error codes) get fragmented into arbitrary subwords by dense tokenizers, losing exact matches.
  • Latency & Pre-computation: Pure on-the-fly dense re-embedding of entire dynamic corpora adds unnecessary CPU latency.
  • The Solution — Hybrid RRF (k=60) + Cross-Encoder Reranking: Chronexa fuses BM25 with disk-backed FAISS vector embeddings, balancing keyword precision and semantic intent, then applies a cross-encoder (ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2) to joint-score top candidate passages.

🛡️ Chronexa's 6 Core Pillars

  • 1. Pre-Retrieval Physical Boundary: Records where timestamp > as_of are excluded before indexing to guarantee 0% future leakage on the benchmark.
  • 2. ISO Timestamp Normalization: Dates like 2026-09-10 are indexed as individual temporal tokens (2026, sep, 10).
  • 3. Header Down-Weighting (0.35x): Message metadata like [Slack #route-planner] is down-weighted so bodies drive relevance.
  • 4. 1.8x Named Entity Boosting: Client codenames ("Acme Freight", "Sarah Patel") are boosted to avoid ambiguous matches.
  • 5. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF, k=60): Dense BGE vectors and sparse lexical BM25 ranks are fused without score calibration issues.
  • 6. Cross-Encoder Joint Reranking: Xenova/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 evaluates full query-document cross-attention on the top 40 candidates, reranking down to the top 8 authoritative passages.
